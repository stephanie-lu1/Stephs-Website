about me

So, what is Cognitive Science anyways?

Humans are producing more data than ever nowadays, and with all of this new information, what is the best way we can actually make sense of it? How can we understand how our minds perceive, learn, reason, and interact with the world? How do we represent concepts that we are so familiar with today? It is not what humans simply know, but more of how we come to know it. From recognizing a face from years and years ago, to understanding language, to making seemingly instinctive decisions, every ordinary aspect of our lives involve the most complex cognitive processes.

So frequently do I get the question of "so what even are you studying?" and it's a completely fair question. I didn't even know the implications of the field until my junior year of college. It is an interdisciplinary field that studies intelligence in both humans and artifical systems. Integrating persepctives from psychology, neuroscience, computer science, linguistics, and philosophy, we work to understand human cognition in all aspects. Not only does it help us understand ourselves more, but it also provides a foundation for developing technologies that can learn from, adapt to, and interact with those who use them.

My Interests

Because Cognitive Science is such a broad area, it is important for me to narrow it down to a few sub-domains that I am passionate about.

As a data scientist, I work with complex and messy datasets to represent and visualize them in the most effective way. Data visualization is not just a technical challenge, but also a design problem. How do we showcase something that is intuitive for the human brain? I want to build digital experiences that leverage this visual processing, while also offering value to whoever is using it. That is where the crux of product design comes from, and what I am focusing on in my Human-Computer Interaction additional major.
